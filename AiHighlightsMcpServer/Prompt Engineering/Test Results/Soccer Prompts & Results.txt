Claude Observations
===================

The headline is that your architecture held up across the whole set — every prompt routed, returned, and rendered without a crash, and the failures 
are all content/tuning failures inside a working frame, not structural ones. That's the dividend of the layering: when "counter-attacks" isn't 
understood, you get a clean "not found," not a corrupted reel. Worth noting explicitly because it's easy to focus on the misroutes and miss that 
the scaffolding passed.

On the routing itself, your tell/make seam diagnosis is confirmed by the data, but the results refine it in an important way: the deciding factor 
isn't the verb so much as whether the model finds matching events. Look at the "best skill" cluster — when it routed to events it found Take On 
and produced a clip; when it routed to Text it declared "no Good skill events." The model is conflating two different concepts ("good skill" the 
event type vs. "best skill" the superlative judgement) and the routing follows whichever interpretation wins that turn. So Group 2's instability is 
partly a routing-description problem (tool-sharpening will help) but partly something tuning can't fix: "best" is a subjective judgement the feed 
doesn't encode, so the model is improvising a proxy (most take-ons). That's worth separating in your mind — some Group 2 wobble is fixable by 
description, some is inherent to asking an event feed a subjective question.

The most significant correctness finding is one you flagged lightly but I'd rank highest: in several tables the named player looks wrong — "best saves" 
attributed to outfield names, and a recurring Brighton/Wolves/Manchester United team-label inconsistency in the build-up tables (Bruno Fernandes 
labelled "Wolves"). This points at something beneath the LLM layer — likely contestant/player-id resolution, or the model picking the wrong participant 
from an event's fields. It matters more than the routing foibles because it's silently plausible: a clip with the wrong name attached looks fine until 
someone who knows the match watches it, which is exactly the "correct, not merely plausible" failure the whole design exists to prevent. I'd treat this 
as its own investigation, separate from routing.

The tool exceptions are the other thread to pull, and your instinct (untried event/qualifier ids) is probably right — but notice the pattern: they cluster 
on the queries probing absent vocabulary (counter-attacks, skill, some saves). That suggests the exception fires on a lookup miss (a qualifierId not in 
the spec or not present in the data) rather than random instability. If so, it's a missing-key path that needs to degrade to "not found" gracefully instead 
of throwing — relevant because a thrown exception mid-reel is a demo-killer even when, as you saw, the answer sometimes survives anyway.

Three smaller notes. Your Group 5 puzzle — PlayerList override returning an event list — is explained by the server stub we knowingly left: the override 
has no teeth yet (it still offers all tools), so "list the goals" routes to events regardless of the combo box. Expected, and it resolves when we give 
the override its single-tool narrowing. The "who kicked off" → Text-instead-of-table case is a genuine routing miss for tool-sharpening's list. And the 
TextResultEnvelope conversion error on the big Bruno query is worth watching: it failed once and worked twice, which smells like the token-ceiling 
truncation again on a very large result — the same length foible, now landing on the text path rather than the events path.

Net: a healthy baseline. The structure is sound; what remains is content correctness (the player/team naming, most urgent), graceful degradation 
(the exceptions), and routing polish (tool-sharpening). Do your refactoring whenever you're ready, and tell me which of the parked items — or which of 
these findings — you'd like to pick up first.
